# Falcons Robotics — Lesson Architecture, Interdisciplinary Curriculum & Navigation

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Required prototype specification  
**Applies to:** Grade 4 first; scalable through Grades 5–12 and localized standards layers.

## 1. Non-negotiable requirement

Falcons must not present lessons as a flat list of unrelated topics. Every learner needs a clear navigation path through a coherent curriculum, with visible links between the real-world problem, scientific principles, mathematics, computing, engineering, design, assessment, project work and demonstrated capability.

Navigation must always answer:
1. Where am I in the learning journey?
2. Why am I learning this?
3. What do I need to know or do next?
4. What evidence will demonstrate mastery?

## 2. Curriculum hierarchy

**Learning Path → Unit / Mission Theme → Real-World Challenge → Interdisciplinary Module → Lesson → Activity / Experiment / Simulation → Project Stage → Assessment → Evidence → Competency → Technology Passport**

Supporting taxonomy:

**Domain → Strand → Competency → Learning Objective → Lesson/Resource → Prerequisite → Assessment Rubric → Evidence Type**

A lesson may map to multiple domains and competencies. Link a lesson once to the knowledge graph rather than duplicating it across subject silos.

## 5. Canonical lesson template

Every lesson record and page follows this recognizable structure. Adapt activity modes to the lesson, but do not omit the core learning and evidence path.

1. **Lesson identity:** title, ID, version, grade context, duration estimate, status and reviewer.
2. **Position:** learning path, unit, mission, previous/next lesson.
3. **Real-world hook:** verified case, observable problem, scenario or question.
4. **Driving question:** meaningful question the learner is trying to answer.
5. **Why it matters:** relevance to people, systems, environment or industry.
6. **Learning objectives:** observable, assessable outcomes.
7. **Success criteria:** what the learner can show or explain.
8. **Prerequisites:** required knowledge/capability, with review links.
9. **Interdisciplinary connections:** each participating domain's concept and why it is needed.
10. **Key concepts and vocabulary:** age-appropriate explanations, diagrams and examples.
11. **Explore / observe:** notice, ask, measure, classify or inspect before being given a solution.
12. **Explain / model:** concise teaching segment connecting evidence to principles.
13. **Guided activity:** worked example, safe demonstration, interactive model or scaffolded practice.
14. **Apply / build / simulate:** learner creates a prediction, model, design or prototype.
15. **Test and collect data:** what to measure, units, method, repeats where appropriate and recording method.
16. **Analyze and improve:** compare results, identify limitations or failures and choose a reasoned improvement.
17. **Explain your thinking:** communicate choices and evidence in written, oral, visual or accessible form.
18. **Check for understanding:** diagnostic questions or practical checks with feedback.
19. **Assessment and evidence:** submission requirements, rubric, evidence type and human review where required.
20. **Reflection / transfer:** what changed, what surprised the learner and where the principle applies elsewhere.
21. **Next step:** next lesson, extension, prerequisite review or capability-matched mission.
22. **Teacher guide:** materials, setup, safety, facilitation, differentiation, misconceptions and answer guidance.
23. **Sources and provenance:** factual references, date checked, confidence/limitations and rights/licensing notes.

Not every lesson requires a physical build or simulation. Select the right activity mode without breaking the lesson structure.

## 6. Interdisciplinary architecture

The curriculum uses a common problem or mission to connect domains. Each connection must be substantive, not merely a list of subject names.

### Core domains
1. **Natural Sciences:** physics; chemistry; biology; Earth and environmental science.
2. **Mathematics & Data:** number, measurement, geometry, ratios, algebraic thinking, statistics, data visualization and uncertainty.
3. **Computing & Digital Systems:** computational thinking, programming, AI, data systems, cybersecurity and digital literacy.
4. **Engineering & Design:** mechanical, electrical, electronics, mechatronics, materials, manufacturing, systems engineering, testing and safety.
5. **Frontier Technologies:** robotics, autonomous systems, drones, space, biotechnology, advanced energy, sensors and emerging fields.
6. **Human, Creative & Society:** design, communication, accessibility, ethics, teamwork, culture, environmental impact and human-machine interaction.
7. **Innovation & Enterprise:** user needs, value, cost, economics, repairability, product design, entrepreneurship and commercialization.

For every contributing domain, state:
- the concept being learned;
- why the mission needs it;
- what the student will do with it;
- how learning will be checked.

Minimum cross-link fields: primary domain, contributing domains, concept links, competency IDs, real-world problem ID, project stage IDs, standards mappings, prerequisite lesson IDs and evidence requirements.

## 7. Example interdisciplinary sequence: Grade 4 — Save Every Drop

**Unit question:** How can we help a small garden use water wisely while keeping plants healthy?

| Lesson | Focus | Interdisciplinary contribution | Student evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Observe the system | Identify problem, users and constraints | Science observation; communication; systems thinking | Problem statement, annotated observation |
| 2. Water and materials | Absorption, drainage, material properties | Natural science; materials engineering; vocabulary | Material comparison record |
| 3. Measure fairly | Measure water volume and compare trials | Mathematics; data tables; fair-test thinking | Baseline measurement table |
| 4. Design options | Sketch and compare solutions | Design; spatial reasoning; constraints | Two sketches and selection rationale |
| 5. Build a safe model | Make a simple water-saving prototype | Engineering design; materials; safe tool use | Prototype and materials list |
| 6. Test and record | Run a fair test and capture results | Measurement; data collection; cause/effect | Test log and simple chart |
| 7. Improve the design | Interpret results and make one change | Systems thinking; iteration; evidence-based decisions | Before/after comparison |
| 8. Explain and transfer | Present solution and limitations | Communication; ethics; environmental impact | Explanation, reflection, portfolio evidence |

A coding/sensor extension is optional and only introduced when prerequisites, safe equipment and teacher supervision are in place. Electronics are not mandatory for the basic Grade 4 pathway.

## 8. Capability-based differentiation

Grade provides context, not a ceiling. Offer multiple levels of support and extension within the same mission:
- **Foundation:** visual instructions, guided choices, vocabulary support and simple observation.
- **Developing:** choose between options, collect measurements and describe patterns.
- **Proficient:** justify decisions with evidence, compare alternatives and control a test.
- **Advanced:** model more variables, automate a measurement or analyze limitations.
- **Inventive:** propose an original, safe variation and defend it with evidence.

Recommend next tasks using prerequisite mastery, evidence and interests—not age alone. Teachers can override recommendations with a recorded reason. Do not lock a learner out of an entire domain based on one score.

## 9. Lesson navigation states

Every lesson must visibly show one state:
- **Locked by prerequisite** (explain why and link to review material)
- **Ready to start**
- **In progress**
- **Submitted / awaiting review**
- **Revision requested**
- **Completed**
- **Mastery verified** (only when defined criteria and required human review are met)

Opening a lesson or watching a video does not mean the learner has mastered it.

## 10. Content and progression rules

- Every unit begins with a meaningful question or real-world problem.
- Teach concepts when they become useful, then revisit with increasing depth.
- Link foundational lessons to the project stage where the concept is applied.
- Include chemistry when materials, batteries, energy conversion, corrosion or water chemistry are relevant.
- Use mathematics for measurement, comparison, modeling and decisions—not only as disconnected worksheets.
- Include computing when algorithms, sensing, data, control or automation add value; do not force coding into every activity.
- Include ethics, safety, accessibility and environmental consequences where relevant.
- Every project has observable test criteria and an evidence path.
- Lessons and generated assets carry version and provenance metadata.
- Localize examples, units, terminology and standards for USA, UAE, Ghana and future regions without fragmenting the shared Falcons core.

## 11. Curriculum Explorer requirements

Support:
- browse by grade context, domain, strand, competency, mission and capability;
- search by topic, problem, technology or learning objective;
- see prerequisites and downstream lessons;
- view a cross-domain map for a selected mission;
- filter by available materials, activity mode and safety constraints;
- inspect related careers, technologies, global problems and projects;
- view standards mapping for the selected jurisdiction;
- see content version and review status.

Use an accessible list/tree as the default. A visual knowledge graph may be an additional view, not the only navigation method.

## 12. Lesson data contract (minimum)

Use typed, validated records rather than unstructured prompt text. Suggested fields:
- identity: id, title, version, status, grade context, order;
- placement: learning path, unit, optional mission, previous/next lesson IDs;
- question and purpose: driving question, real-world hook, why it matters;
- objectives and success criteria;
- prerequisite lesson IDs;
- primary domain and contributing domain records (concept, mission relevance, learner action, check for understanding);
- concept links and competency IDs;
- activity blocks with type, title, instructions, materials and safety notes;
- evidence requirements and rubric ID;
- teacher guide ID;
- source references and standards mappings.

Suggested lesson status: Draft → Review → Published → Retired. Learner progress status is separate: Locked by prerequisite → Ready → In progress → Submitted → Revision requested → Completed → Mastery verified.

## 13. Acceptance criteria

- Learners can identify path, unit, lesson position and next action on every lesson screen.
- Previous/next navigation and prerequisite review work without losing saved work.
- Every published lesson has objectives, success criteria, prerequisites or explicit “none”, interdisciplinary connections, activity steps, a check for understanding and an evidence/assessment path.
- Every cross-domain link explains the concept and its use in the mission.
- Curriculum map links lessons, competencies, projects and evidence consistently.
- Capability adaptations provide meaningful support and extension without equating capability with age.
- Teacher lesson plans include materials, safety, facilitation, misconceptions, differentiation and rubric guidance.
- Search, filters, keyboard navigation and mobile navigation work.
- Progress and mastery states follow defined rules; page views alone never establish mastery.
- Seeded Grade 4 lessons can be traversed end-to-end in the prototype.

## 14. Implementation sequence

1. Build navigation shell, breadcrumbs and progress components.
2. Implement typed curriculum and lesson seed data.
3. Build Curriculum Explorer and canonical lesson template.
4. Implement previous/next and prerequisite states.
5. Link lesson activities to notebook and project stages.
6. Link evidence submission and teacher rubric review.
7. Update Technology Passport only from verified evidence.
8. Add cross-domain map and standards filters.
9. Add curriculum author/reviewer workflow and versioning.
10. Test full navigation on mobile, tablet and desktop before scaling the content library.
